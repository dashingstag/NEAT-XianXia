# Willow Crossing · Real-time village simulation

For the next architecture, see [Million-agent NEAT and emergence plan](SCALE_AND_EMERGENCE_PLAN.md) and its [proposed benchmark specification](scale-benchmark-spec.json). These are planning artifacts; the browser prototype is not yet a million-agent engine.

The recommended implementation stack is documented in [TECH_STACK_DECISION.md](TECH_STACK_DECISION.md): Rust core, Godot viewer, Python experiments, with GPU inference introduced only if profiling supports it.

A standalone top-down continuation of the Xianxia behavior prototypes. The previous two labs remain unchanged. This is a spatial simulation with transparent utility-based decisions; it does not train neural networks or claim to implement NEAT.

**Implemented economy extension:** nine civilian occupations; a selected village leader and quartermaster; public contributions and levies; reserved market orders for food, timber, stone, herbs, tools and medicine; recipe-based crafting and treatment; contingent wages; and supplied, temporary militia duty with exemptions, refusals and equipment returns. See [ECONOMY.md](ECONOMY.md) for rules and a test walkthrough. The lower panel of the simulation contains these controls. **Advance one day** is available for quick experiments.

Run `npm start` in this directory and open http://127.0.0.1:4175/. No dependency installation is needed. Run `npm test` for engine checks. Requires Node 18+ and a modern browser with Canvas. Everything runs locally; no network assets, accounts, persistence, or telemetry. Export downloads a JSON snapshot; import is not implemented.

## Experience

The clearing starts with six people in two caravans. Four additional caravans of two enter at finite scheduled intervals, bringing exactly five food and eight coins per person. Eight living settlers must have reached camp and be nearby together to form Willow Crossing. The threshold can be adjusted before founding. There are at most 14 arrivals, even if people die. No births or endless immigration are simulated.

Villagers independently compare food gathering, timber/stone gathering, hauling, construction, food-for-coin trade, hunting, recovery, and pack retrieval. Every trip takes time across the map. Click a person to inspect hunger, health, energy, traits, inventory, current intention, its reason, and the last ranked alternatives. Click a resource, creature, structure, or dropped pack to inspect it.

Pause, change speed, adjust forest productivity, hill danger, or appetite for risk, and reset with a selected seed. A full simulated day is 180 seconds. Speed changes wall time, not regeneration amounts per simulated second. Hidden browser tabs suspend progress; there is no offline catch-up. The map auto-updates from a fixed 0.1-second simulation step.

## Economy: finite inputs and explicit sinks

All food, timber, stone, coin, and jade has a counted origin. The visible ledger balances:

**initial + imported + renewed + crafted = current + consumed + construction**

Current stock includes environmental nodes, villagers’ packs, warehouse stock, creature loot, dropped goods, market escrow and reserved recipe inputs. Crafting consumes explicit ingredients and records the resulting output separately. The village header shows accessible harvested goods, not all resources still in the wild. Coins move between residents, treasury and reserved orders; they are never minted by work or trade.

- **Forest food:** 18 berry bushes × 7 initial units = 126. Each bush adds one unit per ~83.3 seconds when below capacity, scaled by productivity. Sunlight and moisture are the assumed biological inputs. Ripening stops at seven per bush; there is no pile growing indefinitely.
- **Timber:** 24 trees × 6 units = 144. Partial trees add one timber per ~333.3 seconds, scaled by productivity and capped at six. Fully cleared trees must wait 150 seconds for a sapling before accumulating growth again. Wood is not generated by villagers.
- **Stone:** seven outcrops × 12 units = 84. It never regenerates. The current world therefore has a hard construction-material limit.
- **Hill wildlife:** up to four creatures. Wild boars carry six meat; rare Jadefangs carry ten meat and one jade. On death, those pre-counted goods move into a carcass; someone must carry them back. Existing monsters heal neither themselves nor their loot automatically.
- **Reproduction:** at least 85 simulated seconds between spawns, a vacant wildlife slot, and ten units of habitat forage are all required. Habitat forage starts at 30, is capped at 40, and grows at 0.035 units/second from vegetation, scaled by productivity. This limits sustained reproduction to roughly one creature per 286 seconds at normal productivity after initial reserves are spent. Wildlife-created loot is explicitly recorded as biological renewal, supported by that separate forage pool. Jade is an abstract rare creature product, not mined stone.
- **Food consumption:** hunger rises by 1.15/second. Each actual meal removes one food and reduces hunger by 45. Hunger above 85 damages health, and starvation can kill. Rest does not conjure meals: it uses carried food or an actual warehouse unit. Recovery consumes time and previously eaten nutrition; it does not create tradable resources.
- **Market trade:** sell offers reserve actual stock and buy orders reserve coins. Buyers and suppliers must reach camp to fill one unit, while resident sellers must remain in the village vicinity. Public procurement buys materials; the public store sells crafted tools and medicine; residents offer spare food. Expiry and cancellation return unused reserves. Prices are fixed; dynamic price discovery remains future work.
- **Death:** goods move from the person to a recoverable ground pack. Neither death nor pack retrieval creates or deletes resource units.

## Settlement and construction

Before founding, people can forage and hunt but cannot construct permanent buildings. After founding, material gathering responds to the next building’s costs. Carried goods must be deposited at camp before they can pay construction costs.

The first house costs 16 timber + 4 stone and 24 person-seconds of labor. A market follows for 24 timber + 8 stone and 36 person-seconds. Further houses are built until there are four beds per house for the living population. Several people can contribute labor simultaneously. Costs are removed exactly once, before a project starts; unfinished work already counts as committed construction in the ledger. Houses improve health and energy recovery for up to four resting people each.

In the default seeded world, founding typically occurs within the first simulated minute, followed by material gathering, the first house, and the market. Exact timings can vary with seed and fixed step. Long-term survival is not guaranteed: finite stock, finite immigration, food demand, and capped regeneration can push villagers into dangerous hunting or cause starvation.

## Hill danger

Hunting applies damage to both hunter and creature, consumes energy, and slightly improves combat power after successful kills. Low-health hunters try to disengage. Rare Jadefangs attack travelers within 65 units, including retreating people. Disengagement is not guaranteed safety. Hill danger changes incoming damage. Bravery, food pressure, travel distance, and perceived combat risk influence the decision; physically possible reckless choices remain allowed.

Creatures normally stay within their territories. The optional approaching-threat test makes an existing creature move toward camp without creating loot or a new creature. Militia can defend against nearby predators. There is no terrain collision or pathfinding. Multiple hunters can attack the same creature; authoritative execution prevents duplicate extraction and loot.

## Architecture and limitations

`engine.mjs` owns the world, movement, basic actions, ecology, settlement and conservation. `economy.mjs` owns jobs, council, orders, crafting, clinical work, wages and militia. `app.js` renders the map and controls. `tests.mjs` and `economy.tests.mjs` validate the core and economy. Drawing randomness is separate from simulation randomness. The server serves an explicit file allowlist.

The architecture follows **perception → needs → candidate policy → feasible task → committed trip → consequences**. It adapts the earlier lab to a concrete resource economy. There are no labeled moral hidden nodes, crossover, or online evolutionary generations in this world. A future neural policy can replace utility ranking while retaining the authoritative movement, inventory, feasibility, and ledger layers. Housing, food, wildlife, trade and mortality provide measurable outcome signals for that future experiment.

No cultivation progression, life-state capabilities, farming, births, seasons, weather, ownership disputes, or saving/loading is included. Productivity is a simplified biological-input control. Village status persists after losses. Leadership selection is a transparent ranking rather than a democratic voting model. Prices are fixed, contributions are a simple village rule, and wages depend on available treasury funds. Caravans remain individual arrivals with decorative wagons.
