How this world works

People sense needs, compare feasible jobs, commit to a trip, then resolve actual consequences. Civilian occupation, temporary duty and current task are separate.

Eight people must reach camp to found a village. A council then selects a leader and quartermaster and collects two existing coins from each assembled resident for public work.

A house uses 16 timber + 4 stone. A market uses 24 timber + 8 stone. Construction and crafting reserve inputs before work. All market offers reserve actual inventory or funds.

Artisans turn 2 timber + 1 stone into a tool, or 2 herbs + 1 food into medicine. Tools wear out after 12 work uses. Healers consume medicine to treat patients.

Call-ups require equipment and provisions, exempt officers and essential workers, and depend on trust and willingness. Militia stop civilian jobs and return unused issued gear when stood down.

The hill has wild boars and rare Jadefangs. Hunts cost energy and health. Predators attack nearby travelers. Dead villagers leave recoverable packs.

Food remains finite. Crops are not planted automatically. This is a utility-policy simulation; neural evolution is not running here.

Civilian responsibilities

Jobs change preferences, not physical rules. People can still eat, recover, or take other needed work.

Temporary militia

Needs one tool and two carried food per recruit. Existing personal supplies count. Officers, two foragers, and a healer and artisan are exempt. Low trust can lead to refusal.

Draws an existing hill creature toward camp. Creates no new creature or loot.

Funded orders & reserved offers

Buy orders lock the buyer’s coins. Sell offers lock the seller’s goods. Residents travel to the market to exchange one unit; expired offers return their reserves. Fixed prices in this first version.
